Governed AI Execution

AI agents propose. Governance decides.

In a regulated enterprise, an AI agent must not just receive an API key. Nitrotick makes agents bounded actors: every action is authorized by capability and policy, gated by humans where it matters, and recorded with the exact context that produced it.

Principles

Autonomy with accountability

The question is no longer whether AI agents will run business processes — it is whether you can prove, years later, why each action was allowed.

Bounded actors

Agents operate with explicit, capability-scoped authority. They cannot exceed the scope, duration or risk level they were delegated.

Propose, never decide

Document extraction, anomaly flags and recommendations are proposals. The governed execution layer — not the model — holds authority.

Human approval where it matters

You declare in the workflow definition which steps require human sign-off, from whom and under what conditions.

Decision context is evidence

Each action records the historical context, inputs, policy version and capability it relied on — deterministically reconstructable.

Instant, atomic revocation

If an agent or an external partner's agent loses authorization, its grants are revoked atomically — without stopping healthy processes.

Agent-to-agent across institutions

Agents can delegate across organizational boundaries through Nitrotick Federation, with every hand-off signed and verified.

Example

A governed engineering-to-quality hand-off

Many critical hand-offs still run on e-mail, spreadsheets and manual sign-off chains — workable, but without shared evidence or automatic policy enforcement.

With Nitrotick every arrow becomes a governed transition with its own identity, approval requirement and entry in the evidence chain. AI assists at each step; people approve where you decide; the record is provable on both sides of every boundary.

  1. Engineering change requestAI extracts affected parts and flags risk; the request is submitted under the engineer's identity.
  2. Supplier validationA federated supplier node validates under its own authority and returns signed evidence.
  3. Manufacturing approvalDesignated as critical — held for human approval with full context.
  4. Tool releaseReleased only if the capability and policy version are still valid at execution time.
  5. Quality confirmationFinal state committed; the complete chain is independently verifiable.
