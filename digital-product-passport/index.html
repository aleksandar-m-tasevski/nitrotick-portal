Regulatory context

The EU is making product data verifiable

Under the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) and sector rules such as the EU Battery Regulation, Digital Product Passports become a market-access requirement — product group by product group.

Manufacturers, importers and suppliers must be able to show what was recorded about a product, where it came from and which version is valid — across supply chains, over the product's whole lifecycle.

Passport granularity

Not every passport is the same. Detail defines difficulty.

EU product rules define the level at which a passport applies. The finer the level, the more the passport depends on proving what happened — not just displaying data.

Level 1

Model level

One passport for every identical product of a model.

Example: a basic textile or furniture model. Static product data is often enough.

Level 2

Batch level

Each production batch has its own passport.

Example: food, wine or garments from different fibre lots — each batch with its own suppliers, materials and dates. Needs verifiable lineage.

Level 3

Item level

Every single item has a unique passport and lifecycle.

Example: batteries and automotive parts — service, repair, second life and recycling over many years. Needs verifiable execution.

FAQ

Digital Product Passport questions

Do we need to replace our GS1 barcodes or QR codes?

No. Nitrotick works with your existing GTIN and GLN identifiers and GS1 Digital Link QR codes. Printed codes stay valid — Nitrotick adds a verifiable history behind them.

Is our product data published on a blockchain?

No. Your data stays in your own systems. Nitrotick records versioned state and execution evidence and produces cryptographic proofs that can be verified independently — without tokens, gas fees or a public ledger.

Which products need a Digital Product Passport first?

Under the EU Battery Regulation, electric-vehicle, industrial (above 2 kWh) and light-means-of-transport batteries need a battery passport from 18 February 2027. Under ESPR, requirements arrive product group by product group through delegated acts; textiles and iron and steel are among the first priorities in the European Commission’s working plan.

Do our suppliers have to share their data with us?

No. With Nitrotick Federation each supplier keeps its own data and systems and contributes signed confirmations — for example a material certificate or a lab result. Only the evidence the passport needs is shared.

Can AI fill in the passport automatically?

AI extracts values from specifications, certificates and lab reports and proposes them with a source reference. Nitrotick checks each proposal against policy, routes it to human review where you require it, and records who approved which value and when.

Can we start with one product line?

Yes. Most companies start with one product line on their existing systems and extend to more products, suppliers and markets once the pilot proves its value. Nitrotick runs as SaaS, in a private cloud or on-premise.
