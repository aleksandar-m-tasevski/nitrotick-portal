The problem

A file proves little about its history

When designs, algorithms, research records and technical documents change hands, organizations need more than a file date or an audit-log entry. They need verifiable evidence of what was registered, which version was approved and what can be independently checked later.

Imagine an algorithm developed in 2026. In 2031 an investor, acquirer, auditor or legal expert asks about one historical version. The original team may have moved on, the repository may have been migrated and the original infrastructure may no longer run. The evidence has to stand on its own.

Why the usual evidence falls short

  • Git history aloneIdentifies content inside Git's own data model. It is not independent attestation of when an artifact existed or who approved it.
  • File dates and ZIP archivesEasy to change, and they say nothing about the bytes that came before.
  • Audit logsOwned and editable by the same operator whose records are in question.
  • A single file hashProves that bytes match — but not how versions relate, who approved them or what state they belong to.
How it works

Register. Govern. Commit. Verify.

Four steps turn a valuable artifact into a verifiable history — built on the same durable execution and proof foundation that runs every Nitrotick workflow.

  1. 1 · RegisterSubmit an artifact or a reference to client-owned storage. Nitrotick records a cryptographic fingerprint of its exact bytes and the proposed metadata.
  2. 2 · GovernEnterprise identity, review and approval policies decide which version changes are accepted — with explicit human approval before anything is committed.
  3. 3 · CommitThe approved state is recorded as a new version linked to its predecessor, and a cryptographically signed Nitrotick Proof is issued.
  4. 4 · VerifyValidate the proof and its commitments, and reconstruct the committed state of any historical version when the inputs are available.
Content identity

What the bytes were

A cryptographic fingerprint of the exact uploaded bytes identifies each artifact. Change a single byte and the artifact no longer matches its registered fingerprint.

State commitment

What was approved, and in which order

Each version commits its approved attributes and related document digests, and links to the version before it. Verification can recompute that commitment from the retained inputs and compare it with the original.

Capabilities

Verifiable provenance for high-value intellectual property

Every capability is labelled with its current status, so you know exactly what is available today and what is on the roadmap.

Available

Cryptographic proofs

Every committed version receives a signed Nitrotick Proof that binds its content digests, approved attributes and state commitment.

Available

Version lineage

Each new version points to exactly one predecessor, forming a linear, gap-free history. A forged parent link fails verification.

Available

Human approval

Nothing becomes evidence until an authorized reviewer approves it. Reviewer, approver and uploader are recorded as distinct roles.

Available

Historical state reconstruction

Recompute the committed state of an earlier version and compare it with the recorded commitment — instead of trusting a database administrator.

Available

Client-controlled storage & retention

Pluggable storage backends and governed retention policies, so artifacts can stay in infrastructure you control under your retention rules.

Trust statement

Exactly what is proven — and what is not

Nitrotick verifies evidence of registered data and recorded decisions. It does not itself determine legal ownership, authorship, patentability or legal admissibility.

Scope of Nitrotick IP evidence
EvidenceWhat it establishesWhat it does not establish
Content digestThe supplied bytes match the registered artifactWho created the artifact
Version lineageWhich approved version preceded whichThat no other copies exist elsewhere
Approval recordWhich identity approved the change, under which policyLegal title or inventorship
Nitrotick ProofThe committed state has not been altered since it was provenAn external, qualified time attestation (planned add-on)
State reconstructionThe retained evidence is consistent with the original commitmentThe original file, if it was lawfully deleted under your retention policy

Ownership and authorship claims are recorded as claims, together with the claimant and the basis of the claim. Legal ownership depends on contracts, employment arrangements, assignments and applicable IP law.

FAQ

IP Evidence & Verification — frequently asked questions

Is Nitrotick IP Evidence a patent registration service?

No. Nitrotick creates and verifies technical evidence about what was registered, which version was approved and how it evolved. It does not register IP rights, and it does not determine ownership, authorship, patentability or legal admissibility.

Does Nitrotick store my original files?

That depends on the contracted deployment. Client-owned storage is a supported design target: your artifacts can stay in your own storage while Nitrotick records their content digests, approved metadata and proofs. The exact custody model is defined in your contract.

Can the evidence be verified without Nitrotick online?

A portable evidence bundle and a separately deployable, offline-capable verifier are on the product roadmap. Until they pass their acceptance tests, verification and reconstruction run in the Nitrotick platform.

Is blockchain required?

No. Nitrotick Proofs do not depend on a blockchain. Public-chain anchoring is an optional, planned extension for organizations that want an additional public commitment.

Does AI decide what becomes evidence?

No. AI-assisted metadata extraction is optional and never authoritative. Only attributes approved by an authorized person under your enterprise policy are committed to the evidence.

Is a qualified electronic timestamp included?

Qualified electronic timestamping (eIDAS QeTS) is a planned, optional integration. It will be offered only after the provider integration and its validation are complete, and every proof will clearly state whether an external timestamp is present.
