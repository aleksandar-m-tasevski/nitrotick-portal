The problem

QA lives in spreadsheets. Customers ask for proof.

Inspections, lab results, label approvals and batch decisions are spread across paper forms, e-mail and spreadsheets. When an auditor, a retailer or a regulator asks what happened to one batch, the answer takes days to reconstruct.

What that costs you

  • Slow recallsFinding every affected lot, supplier and customer is manual and error-prone.
  • Unattributable approvalsIt is unclear who approved a label, a lab result or a release — and under which procedure version.
  • Records that can be changedA spreadsheet or shared folder offers no evidence that a record was not edited afterwards.
  • Outdated information on the packPrinted information cannot change when a batch is put on hold.

GS1 Digital Link

One code. The right information for every audience.

A GS1 Digital Link connects your existing GTIN — and, where needed, the batch — to a web address on your own domain. The destination can change without reprinting the pack.

  • ConsumersApproved product information, ingredients, allergens, usage instructions and sustainability.
  • Business partnersSpecifications, certificates and trade data behind authenticated access.
  • QA teams and auditorsBatch status and evidence in role-based portals — confidential reports stay private unless approved for disclosure.
  • SystemsMachine-readable product and batch data for partner applications.
Available

Governed publication

Only information approved in a QA workflow is published, and only the fields classified as public.

In development

GS1-conformant resolver

A resolver tested against the published GS1 conformance suite, running on your domain. Passing the suite is not GS1 certification or retailer approval.

GS1 Sunrise 2027 is an industry goal for retail scanners to read 2D barcodes — not an EU law or a deadline for manufacturers. Existing linear barcodes may still be needed.

Honest evidence

What the evidence proves — and what it does not

It shows

  • Who recorded and who approved each decision
  • Which version of the procedure applied
  • That the record has not been altered since it was approved
  • The full history of each batch, independently verifiable from an export

It does not, on its own, prove

  • That a laboratory result was scientifically correct
  • That an inspection was performed properly
  • That a product is safe
  • Legal compliance or audit acceptance

FAQ

QA Governance & Product Traceability — frequently asked questions

Can a GS1 Digital Link QR code stop a recalled product from being sold?

Not on its own. Scanning the code shows the current approved status, such as HOLD or RECALLED, on every lookup. Blocking a sale at the checkout requires a separate integration with the retailer's point-of-sale systems.

Is a GS1 Digital Link the same as an EU Digital Product Passport for food?

No. Food and feed are excluded from the general EU Ecodesign (ESPR) framework, so a GS1 Digital Link does not create a mandatory Digital Product Passport for food. It is a standards-based way to give consumers and partners verified product information, and physical labelling rules still apply. For products that do fall under ESPR, see Digital Product Passport.

Does verifiable evidence prove that a product is safe?

No. Evidence shows who recorded and approved what, under which process version, and that the record has not been altered since. It does not prove that a laboratory result was scientifically correct or that an inspection was performed properly.

Do we have to replace our ERP, LIMS or QMS?

No. Existing systems stay in place. Nitrotick connects to them through scoped, read-only integrations and adds the governed QA workflow, evidence and publication layer.

Who controls the GS1 Digital Link domain?

You do. The domain encoded in your QR codes belongs to your company, so you can change providers later without reprinting packaging.
