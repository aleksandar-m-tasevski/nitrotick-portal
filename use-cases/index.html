Wine exporter

Macedonian wine — e-labels importers trust

Since December 2023, wine sold in the EU must declare nutrition and ingredients, which producers may provide through an e-label QR code. Importers increasingly also want provable origin and lab values for each batch.

  1. Vineyardharvest and origin
  2. Bottlinglot linked to the GTIN
  3. Lab analysisvalues confirmed by the lab
  4. E-labelscanned in the EU
  5. Verifiedorigin and values proven

Winery gains: an EU-ready e-label with provable origin and stronger trust with importers.

Importer gains: origin and lab values it can verify with one scan — without chasing documents.

Supplier to EU manufacturers

Automotive supplier — prove quality without exposing know-how

European OEMs ask every tier for traceable material, batch and quality data. Sharing process parameters or supplier terms is not an option.

  1. Raw materialcertificate from a GLN-identified supplier
  2. Productionbatch and process version
  3. Qualityreleased by QA
  4. Deliverycustody to the EU buyer
  5. Buyer verifiesevidence, not raw data

Supplier gains: keeps contracts with OEMs that demand traceable data — and keeps its know-how.

OEM gains: independently verifiable batch and quality evidence for its own compliance.

Food & beverages

Food producer — recall in minutes, not days

When one ingredient lot is contaminated, producers without precise lineage recall far more than necessary — and still struggle to prove what happened.

  1. Ingredientssupplier lots confirmed
  2. Productionlots mapped to batches
  3. Issueone lot affected
  4. Tracethe exact affected GTINs
  5. Targeted recallonly what is affected

Producer gains: faster, smaller and cheaper recalls, backed by proof.

Inspector gains: a verifiable record of which lots and products were affected, and when.

Textiles & apparel

Textile manufacturer — sustainability claims that hold up

Textiles are among the first product groups prioritised under ESPR, and green claims face growing scrutiny in the EU. Recycled content and origin must be provable, not just stated.

  1. Fibrerecycled or certified source
  2. Suppliersigns the certificate
  3. Garment batchlinked to the GTIN
  4. Passportcomposition and content
  5. Verified claimno greenwashing

Manufacturer gains: claims that hold up with EU brands, retailers and authorities.

Brand gains: supplier evidence it can rely on for its own product passports.

Batteries

Battery passport — one history from cell to second life

From 18 February 2027, electric-vehicle, industrial (above 2 kWh) and light-means-of-transport batteries need an item-level battery passport. Each battery changes owners and state over many years.

  1. Materialscell and material origin
  2. Packserial linked to the passport
  3. In serviceinstalled in a vehicle or system
  4. Maintenancestate-of-health updates
  5. Second liferepurposing or recycling

Manufacturer gains: one passport that follows each item across owners, with every update evidenced.

Recycler gains: a trusted history for second-life and recycling decisions.

Metals & industry

Steel and aluminium — carbon data buyers can verify

Under CBAM, EU importers of steel, aluminium and other covered goods must report the embedded emissions of what they buy — so they ask producers for precise, batch-level data they can rely on.

  1. Input materialore or scrap origin
  2. Productionprocess and energy per batch
  3. Emissionscalculated under a known method
  4. Certificatemill certificate and passport
  5. Importer verifiesevidence, not raw process data

Producer gains: stays the preferred supplier as EU buyers tighten carbon reporting.

Importer gains: emissions data with a verifiable origin for its own declarations.

Cross-organization execution

Decisions across institutions — without surrendering sovereignty

Each organization runs its own node. Only signals, claims and signed evidence cross the boundary — never raw records.

Banks & fintech

Cross-institution decisions — authorized before value moves

Payments, credit decisions and fraud signals increasingly involve partner institutions and AI models. Each institution must keep its customer data — and prove later why every action was allowed.

  1. Requestfrom a partner institution
  2. Authorityre-verified at execution time
  3. Signalscoped, no raw records
  4. Executiondeterministic, under 100 ms
  5. Evidencesigned on both sides
  6. Settlementonly after proof

Institutions gain: shared decisions and instant revocation without sharing customer data.

Auditors gain: the ability to reconstruct, years later, why each decision was allowed.

Insurance

Leads, renewals and claims — across partners, with provable authority

Insurers work with agents, brokers, banks and partners. Handing customer records to every partner creates risk; e-mail and spreadsheets leave no evidence.

  1. Leadfrom a bank or agent
  2. Delegationscoped and time-limited
  3. Partner actsquote, renewal or claim step
  4. Revocationinstant, if authority ends
  5. Evidenceevery hand-off signed

Insurer gains: wider distribution without handing over customer records.

Partner gains: clear, provable authority for every action it takes.

Manufacturing & aerospace

Engineering change — governed hand-offs across sites and suppliers

Engineering changes still move through e-mail and sign-off chains. With AI assisting at each step, every decision must stay authorized and reconstructable.

  1. Change requestAI flags affected parts
  2. Supplier checkvalidated on the supplier’s node
  3. Approvalheld for a human decision
  4. Tool releaseonly if authority is still valid
  5. Qualityfinal state committed

Manufacturer gains: faster hand-offs with policy enforced at every step.

Customer and auditor gain: a complete, independently verifiable chain of decisions.

Government & public sector

Inter-agency decisions — sovereign, yet provable

Agencies must cooperate on citizen cases without copying each other’s registers — and every decision must stand up to audit.

  1. Citizen requestsubmitted once
  2. Agency Achecks its own register
  3. Signalconfirmation, not a data copy
  4. Decisionunder a known policy version
  5. Evidencekept by both agencies

Agencies gain: cooperation without giving up control of their registers.

Citizens and auditors gain: decisions that can be verified, not just trusted.
